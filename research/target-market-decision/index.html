Research/Unit 3

Target Market Decision

Primary Target:

Adults 21-34 who drink once a week or less, still go out with friends to bars and restaurants, and live in or near an active bar/restaurant scene. We launch first in states where a 0.0% canned cocktail is easy to sell and stock (

Target Strategy:

We will focus on one segment, Moderating Explorers aged 21-34, with one marketing mix. Ch. 9: concentrated marketing can create a competitive advantage over firms trying to reach multiple markets-the right fit for a new brand with a small budget.

Connection to Carlos:

Carlos is the face of this segment. He cut back on drinking for his health, still goes out with friends, wants a drink that is not too sweet, and trusts bartenders, creators, and friends more than ads. Our primary segment is "a city full of Carloses."

Alternative Considered:

Fully alcohol-free adults, 25-50 (our original Unit 1 customer: driving, remission, religion, medication). Meaningful but smaller and harder to reach as one group: loyal if won, but many avoid bar settings. Decision: alternative/phase two.

Size and Purchasing Power

About 64.4 million Americans are aged 21-34 (U.S. Census Bureau, July 2024 estimates). Nationally, the drinking rate hit a record low of 54% (Gallup, 2025).

Profitability

93% of non-alcoholic buyers also buy alcohol (NIQ). Off-premise NA sales reached about $823 million in 2024, up 27.2% (NIQ via ANBA).

Marketing Capabilities

We are a small brand that cannot match Diageo-backed budgets (Unit 1 SWOT). Partner bartenders, creators, and #SipTheWorld are affordable ways to reach this segment.

THE DECISION WE CHANGED

Original Assumption:

Unit 1: our primary customers were adults roughly 25-50 who cannot or choose not to drink, reached first through our own online store.

Research Evidence:

Unit 1 SWOT: people are moderating, not quitting. 93% of NA buyers also buy alcohol (NIQ). Only 50% of 18-34s drink and 66% think moderate drinking is unhealthy (Gallup, 2025). Unit 2 journey map: the key moment is the bartender exchange: 72% of cocktail drinkers ask bar staff when unsure (CGA by NIQ).

Final Decision:

Narrow to moderating drinkers aged 21-34 and lead with partner bartenders in bars and restaurants. The online store becomes the repeat purchase channel, not the launch channel.

Strategic Rationale:

Concentrated targeting gives a small brand a defensible niche. Moderators are already in bars spending money, and the bartender channel matches how Carlos decides. Abstainers stay as a phase two segment.

Which Customers Should We Prioritize?

Ar0und should prioritize adults 21-34 who drink once a week or less but still go out with friends. They are a large group (about 64 million). They drink less than older adults and are more likely to see even moderate drinking as unhealthy, they already spend on nights out, and they decide the way Carlos does: on taste and on the word of a bartender or friend. That is the strongest opportunity for a small, bartender-led, global recipe brand.
