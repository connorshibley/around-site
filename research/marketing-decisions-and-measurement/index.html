Research/Unit 3

Marketing Decisions & Measurement

3 Key Measures

1. Bartender Lift

We see the likelihood of recommendations from bartenders vs. the likelihood of purchase from a grocery or liquor store shelf. In our findings, we had 5 responses for "very likely" to get a drink a bartender recommends, compared with 0 "very likely" responses for grocery/liquor store influence on purchase.

This supports the idea that bartender lift is important for getting new customers to try Ar0und for the first time over just supplying the store's stock.

2. On-Premise Reach

61.9% of our responders reported that they go to a bar or cocktail restaurant at least 1-3 times a month at the minimum. On-Premise reach is going to be influential for our brand, as many seem to be going out to bars/cocktail restaurants frequently.

3. Repeat Channel Mix

Our highest response for most likely to repeat purchase a non-alcoholic beverage was "At bar or restaurant" with 54.8%. Following that was 28.6% for "At the grocery store".

With our highest percentage of respondents being likely to repeat purchase on-premise, like before, we want to focus much of our marketing energy towards bartenders/restaurants/bars for our brand.

Why this supports our hypothesis:

In our target (the 15 people aged 21-34 who drink once a week or less), the average likelihood to order was 2.47 out of 5 when a bartender recommends it, versus 1.67 on a store shelf: a Bartender Lift of +0.80. 10 of the 15 rated the bartender higher than the shelf, and nobody out of all 51 respondents gave the shelf a 4 or 5. Across all 51 respondents, the lift is biggest for people who go out weekly (+1.31) and smallest for people who go out less than once a month (+0.33).

Why this challenges our hypothesis:

27% of our target go out less than once a month; a bartender-led launch would miss about a quarter of them, and they need another route such as grocery stores or online. Overall interest is also low: only 20% of our target gave the bartender a 4 or 5, so a recommendation helps, but it does not make the sale on its own.

How Results could change our STP decisions:

Segmentation: If 25-34 year old city dwellers respond more strongly than 21-24 year old college students, we must split up our segment into 2 narrower sections.

Targeting: If our sober individual segment responds more strongly than moderating customers, we must revise "The Decision We Changed".

Positioning: Only 20% of all 51 respondents agree that most non-alcoholic drinks are too sweet, while 73% enjoy food and drinks from other countries. So we lead with authentic global recipes and keep "not too sweet" as a secondary message.

Marketing mix and B2B: Our results decide where the budget goes(Bartenders or in-store sampling). Business buyers we approach first will depend on where we are aiming our marketing strategy (Restaurant vs. Grocery Store).

Limitations and Next Research

Our biggest limitation is a convenience sample. 30 of our 51 responses were under 21, almost everyone else is a 21-24 college student in the Northeast, and nobody aged 25-34 answered. People also report what they say they would do, not what they actually buy. Next, we would run an ethnographic study in two or three partner bars, then a small test market using the 90-day B2B trial from Unit 2.
