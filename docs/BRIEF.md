# Ar0und home page brief

Interviewed, with authored decisions labelled. Connor answered through question
cards and by sharing his Google Site, three campaign posters and the logo.
Anything marked **(authored)** is Claude's call, not Connor's words.

## The eight topics

1. **Vibe.** Connor: "professional", drinks with "3d animation similar to
   ciaoenergy.com", and from the look question: "Dark, warm, Ar0und accents".
   References he supplied: Ciao Energy's site, Nate Herk's "10,000 AI websites"
   video, his own three "Taste Mexico / Italy / Japan" posters.
2. **The scroll journey.** Connor chose "Globe delivers the cans": scrolling
   turns the globe from the logo; at Mexico, Italy and Japan the matching 3D can
   rises out and spins. The sections around it are **(authored)**, see the curve.
3. **Energy curve. (authored)** Calm open, a quiet dip, one long loud middle,
   a steady read, a browse, a held close.
4. **Feeling, stage by stage, and the one moment.** The one moment is Connor's:
   the globe delivering the cans. The stage-by-stage feelings are **(authored)**.
5. **One thing no other site does.** Connor's pick: the logo's globe hands you a
   drink from wherever it stops. **(authored)** extension: it ends by becoming
   the "O" in AROUND.
6. **Range from premium-minimal. (authored)** Premium-minimal and cinematic,
   following "professional" and the Ciao reference.
7. **One world or distinct scenes.** One trip around one globe (Connor's pick),
   built as acts over a single fixed 3D stage, not as video worldflight.
8. **Assets.** Logo screenshot, three campaign posters, Google Site copy. Flat
   label files, flavour profiles and prices are promised but not yet received.
   No generated imagery.

## What this is and who it is for

Ar0und, "Sip the World": non-alcoholic canned cocktails built on traditional
drinks from around the world. For adults who want a real cocktail without the
alcohol. From the Google Site persona: "He doesn't want a mocktail. He wants an
exciting drink that happens to be alcohol-free."

**What the visitor must believe by the end:** this is a real cocktail from a real
place, and it happens to have no alcohol in it.

**The one action:** "Shop the cans". Same label everywhere.

## Grammar

Filmic one-shot. One linear trip around the world is one argument and one arc.
Why the other seven lost: continuous world requires pre-rendered video flights;
rhythmic cutlist bans pinning and the globe needs a held stage; gallery/catalog
bans a hero claim; chaptered editorial bans a full-bleed hero; live surface,
typographic poster and split stage do not describe a product journey.

## The peak

"You scroll and the world turns, and wherever it stops it hands you a can."
It lives in act 3, which has the largest scroll span on the page. Act 2 before it
is the quietest act: the globe is dimmed and only three lines of type move.

## Tell-someone sentence

"It's the site where you spin the world and it hands you a drink from wherever
you stop."

## Signature move

The globe delivering cans, ending as the logo's "O". Bespoke three.js in
`src/scripts/scene/`, driven by the acts' scroll positions. The Scrollcraft
engine is untouched.

## Authored silence

Between landings in act 3 there is no copy on screen while the globe turns to
the next country. That is deliberate: the turn is the content.
